## Maren Voss — the magician

Born late in the Sundering's earlier decades to a household maintaining a shared bakehouse. His mentor, a non-Fire municipal heatkeeper, teaches him to read cracks, drafts and changing fuel quality; practical skill is not exclusive to affinity. As an apprentice he watches a failed supply agreement leave neighbors without winter heat. He becomes a capable human Fire magician through instruction, repetition and public service, not bloodline or divine selection. Proposed age at signing: 46, born relative year -46, compatible with the candidate -80 to -1 Sundering and year 0 Pact. Exact date remains open.

Temperament: patient with mechanisms, impatient with committee deliberation; dry humor, records every repair, listens poorly when convinced he has the answer. Wants no one to die from a preventable outage; fears helplessness and fragmentation. Flaw: believes concentrating control and maximizing kiln output will guarantee safety. Moral boundary: will not deliberately burn homes or punish a family for its political loyalties. He initially underestimates indirect harm from withholding heat. He knows how to shape, sustain and contain flame but cannot generate endless fuel, heal every wound, control Water magic by default or solve allocation alone. In combat terms Fire remains burning/pressure and bounded burst; civic abilities are narrative concepts, not approved cards.

His visual storytelling brief: travel-worn layered work coat over protective clothing, patched heat-resistant gloves, small record tablet and practical staff shaped by his trade; no crown, permanent flame halo, generic red robe or borrowed Rowan model. A repaired glove from the bakehouse reminds him that expertise is learned from others. Silhouette, body, face, colors and rig remain art review candidates under AV-3D-001 and Character Forge; no production asset.

## Avarn — the Fire Sovereign

An independent mythical being dwelling along ancient thermal corridors. Exact origin, age, reproduction and current whereabouts unresolved. Records describe it before Maren's lifetime; legends say it was born in the first mountain fire, but that is not established truth. It senses heat gradients and disrupted habitats through six broad foot pads. The body is a low, six-limbed basalt-plated tortoise-like form: staggered paired legs, layered refractory plates, recessed furnace-like vent channels, broad blunt muzzle, no conventional dragon wings. Internal chambers exchange heat through a fictional magical process; retain thermal limits, recovery intervals and vulnerability to abrupt cooling. It cannot create infinite energy, predict civic needs or command every flame; Water remains reciprocal opposing affinity, not a moral enemy. No numeric power rank or encounter kit.

It desires a landscape whose heat cycles can recover, fears being reduced to fuel or forced into perpetual service, mistrusts settlements that extract without replenishing. Its flaw is treating all industrial activity as a single harmful pattern; moral boundary: will not knowingly turn inhabited refuges into sacrificial fuel. It communicates by slow articulated gestures, resonant low calls and interpretable heat-pattern signals, not guaranteed telepathy or human speech. Cross-species interpretation requires observation; witnesses may disagree. Proposed movement: deliberate heavy alternating gait, pause to vent, settling rest pose, protective plate-fold, warning rumble and controlled heat release. Distinction from Earth: heat-cycle behavior, vent shutters, thermal pulse timing and material contrast rather than merely rock armor; no real thermodynamic claim. Proposed clips for eventual BART/Blender feasibility: idle, walk, turn, settle, warning, controlled vent, recoil and interaction. Six-limb rig, independent feet and vent controls; no forced human rig. No playable companion, ordinary sale, ownership or hat requirement on an independent divine NPC; if an eligible companion/avatar is separately approved, designated special accessory law applies by meaningful anatomy-specific fit.

## What both gave up

Maren relinquishes unilateral control of fuel and kiln allocation, accepts independent inspection, opens apprenticeship and repair knowledge, and allows emergency decisions to be challenged. He does not surrender his school or powers. Avarn agrees to periodic voluntary assistance and teaching within its recovery limits, accepts managed hearths where communities replenish resources, and risks trusting people who previously exploited its habitat. It can refuse renewed extraction; withdrawal cannot disable magic or veto the Pact. Their accord survives through institutions, alternatives and ordinary labor even when either is absent. Households are participants, not passive beneficiaries. Neither character rescues everyone alone.

### Before they met

Maren's childhood bakehouse is a communal place, not a family monopoly. He is fascinated by the work that happens before a flame is lit: repairing the oven, understanding air and keeping accounts of scarce supplies. His unnamed non-Fire mentor notices his tendency to hurry toward a clever answer without asking who needs it. At -28 he begins training as a Fire practitioner alongside ordinary workers. He develops an unusual ability to control intense local heat without letting it spread, through earned practice. At a candidate workshop emergency before -8, he holds a runaway furnace's heat within a limited reserve while others clear the building and repair its damaged structure. He cannot hold it indefinitely. The rescue succeeds through collective action, but public praise focuses on him; he starts believing that his skill makes him the best judge of other people's needs. His fear of preventable failure hardens into a preference for central control.

Avarn's history is older than Maren's and cannot be dated precisely. Long before their encounter, mortal observers recorded a creature moving between thermal corridors in recurring cycles, letting depleted resting sites recover. Some communities learned to coexist with it; during Dominion-era extraction and Sundering-era shortages, others disrupted those sites. The damage is cumulative, not the work of a single people or new villain. Avarn retreats to a narrower range, losing access to some familiar resting grounds. It comes to expect that any request for assistance will become another demand. It protects itself by avoiding settlements, not by seeking vengeance. Its embodied priorities are legible: rest, territorial memory, recovery and freedom of movement. Its history is not simply a lesson prepared for Maren. Exact age, origin, earlier human contacts and its own name remain open.
